# Why metal feels cold

## Heat moves from warm to cold

Heat always moves from a warmer object to a colder one, until both are at the same temperature. When you hold a cold drink, heat moves from your hand into the can, and your hand feels cold.

## Feeling cold is feeling heat leave

Your skin does not measure the temperature of what you touch. It feels how fast heat leaves your skin. If heat leaves quickly, the object feels cold; if it leaves slowly, it feels warm.

## Conductors and insulators

Some materials pass heat along quickly. They are called conductors; most metals are good conductors. Other materials pass heat slowly. They are called insulators; wood, plastic, wool and still air are insulators.

## The same temperature, a different feeling

In a room, a metal spoon and a wooden spoon are at the same temperature. The metal one feels colder because metal carries heat away from your fingers quickly, while wood carries it away slowly.

## Keeping warm

A wool jumper does not make heat. It traps still air between its fibres, and still air is a poor conductor, so the heat from your body leaves more slowly.
